====================================================================================
RR LYRAE ABSOLUTE MAGNITUDE - the decisive test
====================================================================================

  Real RR Lyrae are horizontal-branch stars at M_W1 ~ -0.5,
  nearly independent of period.

  sample                                                  n   med M_W1   offset
  Pure rr, all                                        6,671      -0.59    -0.09
  Pure rr, confidence >= 0.9                          6,191      -0.62    -0.12
  Pure rr, period_significance > 20                   6,302      -0.61    -0.11
  Extended rr, all                                  221,982       2.51    +3.01
  Extended rr, confidence >= 0.9  (paper's cut)      71,378       2.47    +2.97
  Extended rr, period_significance > 20  (our cut)    7,624      -0.55    -0.05

  => Pure `rr` sits exactly on the RR Lyrae locus, independently
     confirming it is a clean sample.
  => Extended `rr` sits ~3 magnitudes too faint. Those objects are
     not RR Lyrae, regardless of what their periods look like.
  => The paper's confidence cut does not fix this. Cutting on
     `period_significance` returns the sample to the correct locus.

====================================================================================
CAVEATS
====================================================================================

  - Parallax-based absolute magnitudes carry Lutz-Kelker bias; a
    parallax S/N > 5 cut keeps it modest but does not remove it.
    All comparisons here are between subsamples drawn the same way, so the
    bias affects them equally and the *relative* scatter is the meaningful
    quantity.
  - No extinction correction is applied. Mid-IR extinction is small but
    non-zero, and it inflates scatter for objects in the Galactic plane.
  - LPVs are not a single pulsation mode; Miras, semiregulars and overtone
    pulsators occupy different PL sequences, so absolute LPV scatter is
    expected to be large. Again, the comparison between cuts is what matters.
  - Requires a parallax, so this tests only the subset bright and near enough
    for Gaia -- the same brightness bias affecting the rest of this repo.
